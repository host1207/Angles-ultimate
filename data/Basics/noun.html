Chapter 0.2: Nouns (பெயர்ச்சொல்) — The Naming System

Core Definition, The 5 Types of Nouns, and the Countable vs. Uncountable Engine

1. Core Logic: Name = Noun

📘 Fundamental Rule (Koopidrathu / பெயர்): Anything in the universe that we give a name to and call by that name is a Noun.
  • We call a writing tool a pen → pen is a Noun.
  • We call a cloth a towel → towel is a Noun.
  • Role in Sentence Pattern: Only a Noun (or a Pronoun/Gerund acting as a noun) can sit in the Subject (S) or Object (O) slot of a sentence!

2. The 5 Types of Nouns & Their Grammar Behavior

1. Common Noun> Pothu Peyar (பொதுப்பெயர்): A general name given to a wide range of objects or individuals of the same class.> boy, city, pen, towel, student, planet> > boy) & Plural (boys) forms. Takes a/an.> 2. Proper Noun> Sirappu Peyar (சிறப்புப்பெயர்): A specific, unique name given to one particular person, place, or thing. (Anyone can be called a boy, but not everyone can be called Manoj!).> Manoj, Chennai, India, Earth> Singular Proper> Singular Verb.> 3. Collective Noun> team, jury, committee, army, flock, crowd> > The team is); Plural when divided (The team are).> 4. Abstract Noun> love, honesty, advice, knowledge, beauty, courage, anger> > NO Plural form! Never add -s/-es. Always takes a Singular Verb.> 5. Material Noun> gold, iron, water, wood, sand, milk, cotton, plastic> > NO Plural form! (Adding -s changes the meaning completely: wood → woods = forest). Always takes a Singular Verb.>

3. The Master Filter: Countable vs. Uncountable Nouns

⚡ Why This Filter Matters for Exams:
  • Countable Group = Common Nouns + Collective Nouns
    • Can be counted (1, 2, 3...).
    • Can take articles a / an in singular form (a boy, a team).
    • Can be pluralized with -s/-es (boys, teams) and take Plural Verbs when plural.
  • Uncountable Group = Abstract Nouns + Material Nouns
    • Cannot be counted individually (can only be measured in quantity/weight).
    • Can NEVER take a / an (Never say an advice or a gold).
    • Have NO plural form and ALWAYS take a Singular Verb!
