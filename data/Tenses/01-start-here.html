C1: Core Binary Split & Priority Proof

PHRASE

• 0 Finite Verbs inside the block
• Can contain Non-Finite Verbs (V1+ing / V3 / To+V1)

FIRST-PRINCIPLES PROOF

Why "Prepositional Phrase" Exists, But "Prepositional Clause" Is Impossible

(i) Finite Verb Rule: Every Finite Verb [V] requires an active Subject to show tense.

(ii) Preposition Rule: A Preposition always demands an Object (P.O) after it.

(iii) The Subject Crash: Prep forces P.O case → a Subject can NEVER sit after it → Finite Verbs cannot enter!

(iv) Non-Finite Escape: Gerund (V1+ing) needs no Subject & shows no tense → Allowed in Prep. Phrases!

CLAUSE

• 1 Dedicated Subject + 1 Finite Verb [V] (Tense-bearing engine)

MAIN CLAUSE

• Independent core foundation
• Starts with Subject or FANBOYS
• Cannot be classified into Noun/Adj/Adv!
• Tip: Replace embedded Noun Clause with "IT / THIS".

SUBORDINATE CLAUSE

• Dependent meaning on its own
• Starts with Non-FANBOYS Connector
• Branches into 3 Roles:
1. Noun Clause | 2. Adj Clause | 3. Adv Clause

THE 3-STEP "BODMAS" PRIORITY HIERARCHY

Priority 1 (Highest): Finite Verbs [V] lock onto their 1 dedicated Subject & Object first.
Priority 2 (Middle): Clause Boundaries seal around [Connector + Subj + Finite Verb].
Priority 3 (Lowest): Prepositions wake up last & grab whatever unit sits to their right as P.O.

C3: NOUN CLAUSES — THE 4 SENTENCE SLOTS

Every Clause Has Strictly 1 Finite Verb — Stop at the First [V] Boundary!

1. AS SUBJECT (What/Who + [Finite Verb 2])

Sentence: "[What he [said]] [was] true."
• said () = F.V. 1 | was () = F.V. 2
• Why stop at said? Two Finite Verbs = Two Clauses. Clause 1 stops right after said!
• Starts with What → Subordinate Noun Clause (Subject of [was]).

2. AS COMPLEMENT (Subj + State [V] + What?)

Sentence: "My problem [is] [that I [forget] the rules]."
• My problem = Subj | is = State F.V. 1 | forget = F.V. 2
• Question: My problem is what? → [that I forget the rules].
• Replace with "THIS" → "My problem is THIS."

3. AS DIRECT OBJECT (Subj + Action [V] + What/Whom?)

Sentence: "I [know] [who [stole] the ice cream]."
• I = Subj | know () = Action F.V. 1 | stole () = F.V. 2
• Question: I know what/whom? → [who stole the ice cream].
• Direct Object of action verb know → Noun Object Clause.

4. AS PREPOSITIONAL OBJECT (Prep + What/Whom?)

• "You [must pay] attention to [what the teacher [is saying]]."
• "It all [depends] on [how they [perform] tomorrow]."
• Connector (what / how) shields the internal subject; preposition swallows the entire sealed Noun Clause as one P.O!

C5: WHO vs. WHOM LEDGER & ADVERBIAL CLAUSES

Action Verb Object Deduction + Phrase-to-Clause Conversion

WHEN TO USE WHOM (Objective Case)

Sentence: "The boy [whom I [met] yesterday] [is] genius."
• 1. The boy ↔ [is] (Main Clause)
• 2. I ↔ [met] (Subordinate Subject + Action Verb)
• Deduction: [met] already has Subject (I) → asks I met whom? → !

THE 3-VERB DISTRACTOR TRAP (WHO WINS)

Sentence: "The girl [who (you [said]) [won] the prize] [is] my sister."
• 1. The girl ↔ [is] | 2. you ↔ [said] (Distractor)
• 3. who ↔ [won] ([won] is starving for a Subject!)
• An Object (whom) can NEVER be Subject of [won] → MUST be !

• "because + a dog [was chasing] him" = Conjunction + Finite Verb (was chasing) → Adverb Clause
• "because of + a chasing dog" = Compound Preposition (because of) + Non-Finite Participle (chasing) + P.O (dog) → Adverbial Prepositional Phrase!
