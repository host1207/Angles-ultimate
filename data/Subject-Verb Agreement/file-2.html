Subject-Verb Agreement — Part 2

Concepts 7–11: Identical Noun Pairs, Distributive & Indefinite Pronouns, Collective Nouns & Proper Titles

Concept 7: Identical Nouns Joined by a Preposition

📘 Golden Rule: If two identical nouns are joined by a preposition (Noun 1 + Preposition + Noun 1), both nouns must remain Singular in form and the verb must also be Singular.
⚠️ SSC Exam Trap: Examiners love pluralizing the nouns (e.g., "Towns after towns" or "Men after men"). Always force both nouns into their singular base form ("Town after town", "Man after man")!
Town after town plundered.> Door to door service available.> Word for word repetition not allowed.> Men after men have turned up for the ceremony.>

Concept 8: Distributive Words ("Each" & "Every")

📘 Golden Rule: Each and Every are strictly followed by:
Singular Noun → Singular Verb → Singular Possessive Adjective (his/her) → Singular Pronoun
⚠️ SSC Exam Traps:
  • Partitive Trap ("of"): Each is both an Adjective and a Pronoun, so Each of + Plural Noun is VALID. However, Every is strictly an Adjective—it can NEVER be followed directly by of (never write "Every of..."; use "Each of..." or "Every one of...").
  • Post-Subject Exception: If a plural pronoun comes first (We each / They each), the verb follows the plural subject → Plural Verb (e.g., "We each have been rewarded").
Every student very talented.> of my students very talented.> of the five children standing in the corner.> Each part of a banana tree useful.> Each boy done work .> Each boy handed work.> Each boy and each girl done work.> We each been rewarded.>
Each student ordered to complete assignment.> Every soldier expected to be brave.> Each boy and girl punished.> Each boy and each girl punished.> Every man, woman and child excited to perform in the Dandiya competition.>

Concept 9: Indefinite Pronouns (-one, -body, -thing)

📘 Golden Rule: All 12 compound indefinite pronouns ending in -one, -body, and -thing are strictly Singular and always take a Singular Verb.
Everyone present at the party.> Everyone except me present at the party.> Everyone except the students present at the Party.> Someone knocking at the door.> No one taught me English.>

Concept 10: Collective Nouns (Jury, Committee, Army, Team, Audience)

📘 Golden Rule: Collective nouns change agreement based on whether the group acts as one unit or separate individuals:
  • United / Unanimous / General Statement: Singular Verb → Singular Adjective (its) → Pronoun "it"
  • Divided / Per-Person Action Emphasized: Plural Verb → Plural Adjective (their) → Pronoun "they"
The jury taken the decision, hasn't ?> The audience took the seats, didn't ?> The army changed the uniforms, didn't ?> The audience watching the movie.> The army marching towards the Himalayas.> The jury taking a decision.>

Concept 11: Nouns that Look Plural but Are Singular

📘 Golden Rule: Proper names of books, countries, organizations, subjects, diseases, and games that end in -s look plural in form, but represent a single entity and strictly take a Singular Verb.
“The Arabian Nights” indeed an interesting book.> ‘Gulliver’s Travels’ indeed an interesting book.> The US want India in the Security Council.> A Tale of Two Cities written by Charles Dickens.>
