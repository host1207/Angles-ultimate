Concept 18: "The Percentage of" vs. "Percent of"

📘 Golden Rule:
  • The percentage of: "Percentage" is an Uncountable Noun. Regardless of whether the noun after of is singular or plural, The percentage of + Noun always takes a Singular Verb.
  • X% (Percent) of: In sentences with a specific percentage (e.g., 21% of, 50% of), the Noun that follows 'of' decides whether the verb is Singular or Plural.
The percentage of girls less than that of boys.> The percentage of Oxygen less than that of Nitrogen.> the students girls.> the air Oxygen.> people they have not sent a hand-written letter.>

Concept 19: Fractions & Ratios (One-third, Three-fourths)

📘 Golden Rule: With fractions and proportions (One-third of, Two-thirds of, Three-fourths of, The rest of, A quarter of, Part of), the verb agrees strictly with the Noun that follows 'of':
  • Fraction + of + Uncountable / Singular Noun → Singular Verb
  • Fraction + of + Plural Countable Noun → Plural Verb
the syllabus been covered.> the students absent.> the information incorrect.>
the pillar [ has / have ] rotted away.> the men [ has / have ] gone to war.>

Concept 20: Uncountable Nouns (Always Singular)

📘 Golden Rule: Uncountable nouns cannot be pluralized by adding -s/-es, cannot take indefinite articles (a/an), and strictly take a Singular Verb.

Concept 21: Nouns Always Plural in Form & Meaning

📘 Golden Rule: Paired instruments/garments (scissors, trousers, spectacles) and certain collective plural nouns always end in -s, cannot drop their -s, and strictly take a Plural Verb.
Paired Tools & Apparel> Valuables, Places & Legal> Other Plural-Only Nouns>
belongings lost.> scissors for cutting paper.> clothes dirty.> trousers dirty.> spectacles been broken.>

Concept 22: Nouns Plural in Form (Ending in -s) but Singular in Meaning

📘 Golden Rule: Academic subjects, diseases, games, book titles, and words like News / Summons end in -s but are Singular in meaning → Use a Singular Verb.
⚠️ SSC Exam Exception (Subjects vs. Calculations/Data): When academic words like Mathematics, Statistics, or Politics refer to calculations, data figures, or political views (often preceded by My, His, The or used to mean data), they become Plural and take a Plural Verb!
Academic Subjects (-ics)> Diseases> Books & Literature> Games & Sports> Miscellaneous>
Mathematics an interesting subject.> Mathematics telling that he has made a lot of money.> Statistics difficult to understand.> Statistics us that it will rain today.> Politics a game of compromise.> This news broadcast from All India Radio only yesterday.>

Concept 23: Nouns that Look Singular but Are Plural in Meaning (6 Ps & 3 Cs)

📘 Golden Rule: Certain collective group nouns do not end in -s, yet they are Plural in meaning and take a Plural Verb (unless used strictly as an abstract profession/meat/unit name):
  • The 6 Ps: Police, People, Poultry, Peasantry, Personnel (staff), Public
  • The 3 Cs: Cattle, Clergy, Cavalry
  • Others: Electorate, Infantry, Gentry, Vermin, Youth
⚠️ SSC Context Nuances (Slide Breakdown):
  • Never add -s: Never write "Cattles" or "Personnels".
  • People vs. Peoples: People = persons (plural). Peoples = different nations/ethnicities (e.g., "The Greeks were brave peoples").
  • Dual-Context Nouns:
    • Poultry = birds/hens (Plural: "Poultry are culled") vs. meat/food (Singular Uncountable: "Poultry is better than red meat").
    • Peasantry = farmers (Plural: "Peasantry are raising slogans") vs. profession (Singular: "Peasantry is a thankless profession").
    • Vermin = harmful insects/pests (Plural: "Vermin are small insects") vs. collective term for offensive people (Singular in definition: "Vermin means unpleasant people").
    • Infantry = soldiers (Plural: "Infantry were/are taught how to camouflage themselves") vs. single military division (Singular: "Infantry is an essential unit of the army").
Vermin [ is / are ] small insects and animals harmful to plants and crops.> The police [ has / have ] arrived, and the electorate [ has / have ] voted against the party.> The youths needs jobs. (b) Cattles are grazing in the field. (c) Police personnels have arrived.> Local clergy [ has / have ] come to resolve the issue.> [ is / are ] a thankless profession. vs. (b) Peasantry [ is / are ] raising slogans against the government.> [ is / are ] better than red meat. vs. (b) Poultry [ was / were ] culled to check bird flu.> [ was / were ] taught how to camouflage themselves. vs. (b) Infantry [ is / are ] an essential unit of the army.>

Concept 24: Units of Time, Money, Speed, Weight, Height & Distance

📘 Golden Rule:
  • Covered/Used in ONE Unified Way (Single Unit/Total): Takes a Singular Verb.
  • Covered/Spent in VARIOUS Ways (Divided/Itemized): Takes a Plural Verb.
Ten miles to be covered completely on foot.> Ten miles to be covered partly by car and rest on foot.> Five thousand rupees his salary.> Five thousand rupees spent on food and entertainment.> Six weeks not long enough.> Five kilograms his weight.>

Concept 25: Uncountable Nouns That Change Meaning When "-s / -es" Is Added

📘 Golden Rule: Uncountable nouns cannot be pluralized. When -s or -es is attached to them, it does NOT make them a normal plural—instead, it completely changes the meaning of the word!
