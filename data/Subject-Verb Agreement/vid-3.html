Concepts 12–16: Quantifiers (Many / Many a / Number of / Plenty of) & Partitive Selection ("Of" Rule)

Concept 12A: "Many / A Great Many" vs. "Many a / More Than One"

📘 Golden Rule (Structure Breakdown):
  • Many / A great many / A good many + Plural Noun + Plural Verb
  • Many a / More than one + Singular Noun + Singular Verb

Concept 12B: "A Number of" vs. "The Number of"

📘 Golden Rule: Both phrases take a Plural Noun, but the article changes the verb:
  • A number of + Plural Noun → Plural Verb (Means "many/several" acting individually).
  • The number of + Plural Noun → Singular Verb (Refers to one specific numerical count/figure).
A number of boys come.> The number of boys 5000.> A number of tickets sold almost immediately.> The number of employees taking legal action increasing.>
The number of guests invited to the party five hundred.> A number of points of resemblance between the Australian and Dravidian languages discovered.>

Concept 13: "Plenty of" (Countable vs. Uncountable)

📘 Golden Rule: Plenty of adapts its verb to the noun that follows of:
  • Plenty of + Plural Countable Noun → Plural Verb (Never use a Singular Countable Noun!)
  • Plenty of + Uncountable Noun → Singular Verb

Concept 15: Singular Pronouns + "Of" Selection Rule

📘 Golden Rule: When selecting from a group using Each, Any, One, None, Either, Neither, Everyone, Someone:
[Singular Pronoun] + of + Plural Noun / Plural Pronoun → Singular Verb → Singular Adjective (his/her) → Singular Pronoun
⚠️ SSC Exam Double-Check: Whenever you see One of the... or Each of the..., check two places immediately:
  1. The Noun right after of the MUST be Plural (e.g., One of the boys, never One of the boy).
  2. The Verb and Possessive Adjective MUST be Singular (e.g., is doing his work, never are / their).
