3) Reflexive Pronouns (T3)
ST1: Reflexive Pronouns
• Myself — (F.P) (S)
• Ourselves — (F.P) (P)
• Yourself — (S.P) (S)
• Yourselves — (S.P) (P)
• Himself — (T.P) (S)
• Herself — (T.P) (S)
• Itself — (T.P) (S)
• Themselves — (T.P) (P)
* It is used when the doer and receiver is the same
Id1: Identification
• Sits as an object (like after a transitive verb or a preposition).
Trap - 1 (T1)
• If transitive verbs don't have an external object, put a Reflexive Pronoun.
Eg 1: We enjoyed at the party. ✗
We enjoyed ourselves at the party. ✓
Eg 2: Students should avail the library facilities. ✗
Students should avail themselves of the library facilities. ✓
Trap - 2 (T2)
• Verbs that already tell info about the object never take a Reflexive Pronoun.
• Hid is usually done by oneself, so no him/herself after this. Same for prepare, qualify, keep, stop, bathe, move, turn, etc.
Eg: He hid himself behind the tree. ✗
He hid behind the tree. ✓
Trap - 3 (T3)
• Reflexive pronouns will never come as a subject.
Eg 1: Myself Manoj. ✗
I am Manoj. ✓
Eg 2: God and myself will defeat the evil. ✗
God and I will defeat the evil. ✓
Trap - 4 (T4)
• In imperative sentences, the subject ("You") is hidden, so use self-case after the verb in these cases.
Eg: Put you in that position. (Here You put you is wrong) ✗
[After a transitive verb put self-case] → Put yourself in that position. ✓
Trap - 5 (T5)
• When showing physical location prepositions, use objective case instead of self-case.
Eg: He placed his laptop beside himself. ✗
He placed his laptop beside him. ✓
Trap - 6 (T6)
• When an action is shared mutually between 2 or more people, use a Reciprocal Pronoun (each other / one another) instead of a Reflexive Pronoun.
Eg: Both of them blamed themselves. (Self-blame, not mutual) ✗
Both of them blamed each other. ✓

4) Emphatic Pronouns (T4)
ST1: Emphatic Pronouns
• Myself — (F.P) (S)
• Ourselves — (F.P) (P)
• Yourself — (S.P) (S)
• Yourselves — (S.P) (P)
• Himself — (T.P) (S)
• Herself — (T.P) (S)
• Itself — (T.P) (S)
• Themselves — (T.P) (P)
Id1: Identification
• Emphatic pronouns never occupy an objective seat.
• Instead, they sit near a noun/pronoun to emphasize it.
• Deleting it will not break the sentence.
Eg (Emphatic): She herself wrote it for me.
→ She wrote it for me. ✓ (Valid after deletion)
• Reflexive pronouns, on the other hand, actually act as objects, so they can't be deleted.
Eg (Reflexive): She blamed herself for the crash.
→ Here "herself" is the object after a transitive verb, so it can't be deleted. ✗
Trap - 1 (T1)
• An Emphatic pronoun can never come alone as a subject.
Myself will handle the backend. ✗
I myself will handle the backend. ✓
Trap - 2 (T2)
• United Collective Noun → itself
Eg: The committee itself admitted its error in the answer key. ✓
• Divided Collective Noun → themselves
Eg: The jury themselves were divided in their final verdict. ✓
• Generic "One" → oneself
Eg: One oneself must verify the source before sharing the data. ✓
Trap - 3 (T3)
• Placing an emphatic pronoun next to the wrong noun shifts the emphasis onto the wrong person or creates ambiguity.
Subject Stress
The Prime Minister herself inaugurated the facility. (Stresses that it was the PM)
Object Stress
I met the Prime Minister herself. (Emphasizes that I met the actual PM)
Ambiguous Trap
The manager met the intern herself. (Not known / can't be deduced) ✗
The manager herself met the intern. ✓
Trap - 4 (T4)
• Never stack possessives with emphatic pronouns.
Wrong — This is my myself laptop. ✗
Correct — This is my laptop. ✓

5) Demonstrative Pronouns (T5)
ST1: Demonstrative Pronouns & Adjectives
1) This — (இது) Pronoun (Singular) & (இந்த) Adjective
• Pronoun (S): Eg: This is my car. (இது என்னுடைய கார்)
• Adjective (இந்த): [த + அ = எச்சம் — பெயரெச்சம்] → Eg: This car is mine. (இந்த கார்)
2) These — (இவை / இது) Pronoun (Plural) & (இந்த) Adjective
• Pronoun (P): Eg: These are great cars. (இவை)
• Adjective (இந்த): Comes before a plural noun.
3) That — (அது) Pronoun (Singular) & (அந்த) Adjective
• Pronoun (S): Eg: That is my car. (அது)  |  Adjective (அந்த): Eg: That car is mine.
4) Those — (அவைகள் / அது) Pronoun (Plural) & (அந்த) Adjective
• Pronoun (P) & Adjective (அந்த) before plural nouns.
5) That of (Singular Comparison) & Those of (Plural Comparison)
The weather of Chennai is hotter than Bengaluru. (Compares weather to a city) ✗
The weather of Chennai is hotter than that of Bengaluru. (that of = Bengaluru's weather) ✓
• Those of: Used for plural comparison pronouns.
6) Such — Can be Singular / Plural (Used to point at something)
Such was the bugs in the legacy code. ✗
Such were the bugs in the legacy code. ✓
Trap - 1 (T1)
• "These" and "Those" are only plural adjectives, so they also force the nouns they are referring to be plural.
Those guy are strong. ✗
Those guys are strong. ✓
Trap - 2 (T2)
• If there are two items to be pointed, "that" is used to point the former one and "this" to point the newer (latter) one.
Both C++ and Python are popular; while this is older, that is easier to read. ✗
Both C++ and Python are popular; while that (former) is older, this (newer) is easier to read. ✓
Trap - 3 (T3)
• In an adjective clause, when introducing a general group, always use "Those" (never "They").
They who practice win. ✗
Those who practice win. ✓
Trap - 4 (T4)
• "The same" is treated as an adjective (Eg: The same file).
• So "the same" can't function as a noun / pronoun.
Please verify the configuration and upload the same to the server. ✗
Please verify the configuration and upload it to the server. ✓

6) Relative Pronouns (T6)
Id1: Identification
• Relative Pronoun sits after a noun and also acts as a conjunction.
• Relative Pronoun = Pronoun + Conjunction
• Relative Pronouns usually sit on the right of the noun.
• Relative Pronouns which are subjective require a finite verb.
ST1: Who
• Singular / Plural — used in Subjective Case.
• Requires a finite verb.
Her opponent, whom the critics say lacks experience, lost the debate. ✗
• critics → say (F.V)  |  Her opponent → lost (F.V)  |  ? → lacks (F.V) needs Subj. Case, but only has Obj. Case ("whom").
Her opponent, who the critics say lacks experience, lost the debate. ✓
→ Here all finite verbs are met with their subjective case.
ST2: Whom
• Singular / Plural
• Objective Case
• Doesn't require a finite verb.
The witness who the prosecutor believed to be reliable contradicted the suspect. ✗
• The prosecutor → believed  |  The witness → contradicted  |  Who → ? ✗
The witness whom the prosecutor believed to be reliable contradicted the suspect. ✓
ST3: Which
• Singular / Plural  |  Subjective / Objective Case
• Used to refer to things other than people.
Eg: The kite which you gave me was nice.
ST4: That
• Singular / Plural  |  Subjective / Objective Case
• Used to refer to things and sometimes people.
• Special Case Relative Pronoun (R.P).
Used When →
1) Using Superlative Degree
This is the cleanest river which I have ever reviewed. ✗
This is the cleanest river that I have ever reviewed. ✓
2) All-Inclusive / Limiting (all, the same, the only, none, nothing, anything, everything, something)
All which glitters is not gold. ✗
All that glitters is not gold. ✓
3) Mixed Antecedent (Person + Animal / Person + Thing)
The rider and his horse who crossed the finish line won the trophy. ✗
The rider and his horse that crossed the finish line won the trophy. ✓
4) After Interrogative Words
Who is the person who leaked credentials? ✗
Who is the person that leaked credentials? ✓
Comma Ban on "That"
• When a relative clause adds extra non-essential information set off by a comma, "that" is banned — use which or who / whom.
Python, that is dynamically typed, is widely used in AI. ✗
Python, which is dynamically typed, is widely used in AI. ✓
Placements of "That"
As Demonstrative Pronoun
• Takes a concrete subject or object spot and doesn't act as a conjunction.
Eg: That is my car. — (D.P)
As Demonstrative Adjective
• Comes on the left of a noun (or between an article and a noun) and speaks about a noun.
Eg: That car is mine. — (D.A)
As Relative Pronoun
• Comes to the right of the noun as a Pronoun + Conjunction.
• Requires a finite verb.
Eg: The car that you gave me is fast.
As a Conjunction
Eg: She said that she would arrive early.
• "That" as a conjunction never occupies a subject/object position, so it can actually be deleted from a sentence and the sentence will still make sense.
ST5: Whose
• Possessive Adjective + Conjunction
• Doesn't have S / P of its own — depends on the antecedent.
ST6: What
• An R.P which has an antecedent built in.
• Never place a noun directly before "what".
Please send me the document what you edited. ✗
Please send me the document that you edited. ✓
Trap - 1 (T1)
• A Relative Pronoun has no singular or plural number of its own; the finite verb sitting after it must agree with its antecedent.
It is I who is responsible for deployment. ✗
It is I who am responsible for deployment. ✓

9) Interrogative Pronouns (T9)
Id1: Identification
• Used to ask a direct or indirect question (Who, Whom, Whose, Which, What).
1) Interrogative Pronoun vs Relative Pronoun
• Interrogative Pronoun: Has NO antecedent noun before it and governs 1 Finite Verb (F.V) in a simple question → Who broke the server?
• Relative Pronoun: Sits after an antecedent noun as a Pronoun + Conjunction joining 2 Finite Verbs (F.Vs) → The engineer who broke the server fixed it.
2) Interrogative Pronoun vs Interrogative Adjective
• Pronoun: Stands alone before a Finite Verb (F.V) → Which is your laptop?
• Adjective: Sits directly before a noun → Which laptop is yours?
ST1: Who (Subjective Case — Persons)
• Asks about the Subject (Doer) of the action.
• Because "Who" is in the Subjective Case, it requires a Finite Verb (F.V) that lacks its own subject.
Whom authorized this transaction? ✗
• authorized (F.V) has NO subjective pronoun; "Whom" is Objective Case.
Who authorized this transaction? ✓
→ "Who" (Subj. Case) satisfies the subject requirement of the finite verb "authorized".
ST2: Whom (Objective Case — Persons)
• Asks about the Object (Receiver) of a transitive verb or preposition.
• Does NOT require a Finite Verb (F.V) — the Finite Verb in the clause already has its own subject!
Who did the committee select? ✗
• the committee (Subj) → did select (F.V). The finite verb already has its subject!
Whom did the committee select? ✓
→ Since the finite verb "did select" already has its subject ("the committee"), only Objective Case ("Whom") is needed for the object slot.
ST3: Whose (Possessive Case — Persons)
• Asks about ownership / possession.
• Possessive Pronoun (Udayathu): Stands alone before a Finite Verb (F.V) → Whose is this unsigned file?
• Possessive Adjective (Udaya): Sits directly before a noun → Whose signature is on this file?
ST4: Which (Selective Choice — Persons OR Things)
• Used when selecting from a definite, limited, or known group (applies to both people and things).
Who of the two candidates scored higher? ✗
Which of the two candidates scored higher? ✓ ("of the two candidates" = restricted set → Which)
ST5: What (Open Inquiry — Things or Profession)
1) For Things (Unlimited / General Choice)
• Eg: What do you want to read? (Wide open, no restricted list)
2) For People's Profession vs Identity
• Who is he? → Asks for his name / identity (He is Rahul).
• What is he? → Asks for his profession / rank (He is a Senior Architect).
Trap - 1 (T1)
• A preposition always forces the Objective Case (PO) and never takes a Finite Verb (F.V). Immediately after a preposition (By, To, With, For), always use "Whom", never "Who".
By who was the ancient manuscript translated? ✗
By whom was the ancient manuscript translated? ✓
Trap - 2 (T2)
• When an interrogative word joins a clause inside a statement (Indirect Question), the embedded clause must follow normal Subject + Finite Verb (F.V) order — never invert the finite/helping verb before the subject!
Can you tell me what is the root cause of the outage? ✗
Can you tell me what the root cause of the outage is? ✓
Trap - 3 (T3)
• When an interrupting clause like "do you think / suppose / believe" is inserted, count the Finite Verbs (F.Vs) and their Subjects! If a Finite Verb is still missing its Subject, use "Who" (never "Whom").
Whom do you think will win the championship? ✗
• you (Subj 1) → do think (F.V 1)  |  ? (No Subj 2) → will win (F.V 2) lacks a Subject!
Who do you think will win the championship? ✓
→ "Who" (Subj 2) provides the required Subject for the finite verb "will win" (F.V 2).
Trap - 4 (T4)
• "Who's" contains a built-in Finite Verb (Who is / Who has), whereas "Whose" is pure possessive. Never use "Who's" when the clause already has its own Finite Verb!
Who's turn is it to audit the database? (2 F.Vs crash: is + is) ✗
Whose turn is it to audit the database? ✓

10) Reciprocal Pronouns (T10)
Id1: Identification
• Expresses a two-way / mutual action (ஒருவருக்கொருவர்) where Subject A acts on Subject B, and Subject B simultaneously acts on Subject A (A ↔ B).
• Finite Verb & Subject Lock: Because mutual action requires at least 2 participants, the Subject before the Finite Verb (F.V) must always be Plural (or a compound subject like A and B).
• Syntactic Slot Lock: Reciprocal pronouns ONLY sit in Object (O), Prepositional Object (PO), or Possessive Adjective ('s + Noun) slots — never as the Subject of a Finite Verb!
ST1: Each other (Strictly Used for 2)
• Used when a mutual action occurs between exactly 2 people or things.
1) Direct Object of Transitive Finite Verb (F.V)
Eg: Rahul and Priya helped each other.
2) Prepositional Object (PO) after Intransitive F.V + Prep
Eg: The two servers communicate with each other.
3) Possessive Adjective Slot (Each other's + Noun)
Eg: Both developers reviewed each other's code.
ST2: One another (Used for 2+ / 3 or More)
• Used when a mutual action occurs among more than 2 (3 or more) people or things, or a general group.
1) Direct Object of Transitive Finite Verb (F.V)
Eg: All ten team members congratulated one another.
2) Prepositional Object (PO) after Intransitive F.V + Prep
Eg: The five nations traded with one another.
3) Possessive Adjective Slot (One another's + Noun)
Eg: All the students checked one another's notes.
Trap - 1 (T1): Quantity Count (2 vs 2+)
• Count the Subject before the Finite Verb (F.V):
  • Count = 2 (both, two, twins, couple, A and B) → each other
  • Count = 2+ (three, four, all, citizens, players) → one another
The two rival CEOs shook hands with one another. ✗
The two rival CEOs shook hands with each other. ✓
The four finalists competed fiercely against each other. ✗
The four finalists competed fiercely against one another. ✓
Trap - 2 (T2): Never Use as Subject of a Finite Verb (F.V)
• Because a Finite Verb (F.V) requires a Subjective Pronoun/Noun, "each other" and "one another" can never sit before a Finite Verb as its Subject. Split them into each ... the other!
They know that each other is hiding the truth. ✗
They each know that the other is hiding the truth. ✓
Trap - 3 (T3): Reflexive (Themselves) vs Reciprocal (Each Other)
• Both "themselves" and "each other / one another" sit in the Object slot after a Plural Subject + Transitive Finite Verb (F.V), which causes exam traps. Check the direction of action:
1) Reflexive ("themselves") → Individual Self-Loop (A → A & B → B)
• Person A acts on Person A, and Person B acts on Person B (தன்னையே / தங்களையே — no cross-interaction between A and B).
Eg: Rahul and Priya prepared themselves for the interview. (Rahul prepared himself + Priya prepared herself) ✓
2) Reciprocal ("each other") → Cross-Transfer Action (A → B & B → A)
• Person A acts on Person B, and Person B acts on Person A (ஒருவருக்கொருவர் — two-way exchange).
• Exam Trap: Using "themselves" with mutual verbs (help, love, hate, blame in a fight, exchange, greet, marry, argue with, know) changes the meaning to self-action or makes it logically impossible!
Rahul and Priya have known themselves since childhood. (Means Rahul only knows Rahul!) ✗
Rahul and Priya have known each other since childhood. (Rahul knows Priya & Priya knows Rahul) ✓
The two captains exchanged jerseys with themselves. (A person cannot exchange with himself!) ✗
The two captains exchanged jerseys with each other. ✓
Trap - 4 (T4): Possessive ('s) & Preposition Lock
1) Always Use Singular Apostrophe ('s) — Never (s')
• Treat each other and one another as singular compound units → always write each other's and one another's (never each others').
The two partners always respect each others' opinions. ✗
The two partners always respect each other's opinions. ✓
2) Intransitive Finite Verb (F.V) Must Keep Its Preposition Before "Each Other"
The two brothers have not spoken each other for years. ✗
The two brothers have not spoken to each other for years. ✓
